Compress JPG/PNG/WebP locally.

How to use

  1. Drag a JPG, PNG or WebP image into the drop zone.
  2. Set compression quality on the slider — 0.7 is the ideal starting point.
  3. Enable resizing if you want smaller dimensions along with compression.
  4. Compare before/after sizes in the instant preview.
  5. Download the compressed image, or batch-compress everything at once.

Detailed explanation

Image compression is not optional in the modern web, it is mandatory: a modern phone photo weighs 4-8 MB, and a page with three of those forces 20 MB on visitors for nothing — while quality 0.7 cuts size to roughly 10% with visually invisible loss.

The technical secret is JPEG's trade-off: the format discards information human eyes cannot distinguish, and lower quality discards more — the 0.6-0.8 zone is the sweet spot where savings are huge and degradation invisible.

Local compression through the browser canvas means no upload, no server wait, no count limit — hundreds of images flow smoothly on your own machine, an advantage most free sites (which upload your photos) do not offer.

For SEO, image compression is a strong indirect ranking signal: page speed is an official Core Web Vitals metric, and heavy images are its number-one enemy.

Final tip: an image displayed at 800px has no business being 4000px — enable resizing together with compression for the optimal result.

FAQ

What is the best quality value?

0.7 for general photos, 0.85 for product and portfolio images — above 0.9 doubles size for negligible gain.

Is any data permanently lost?

Yes, JPEG compression is destructive by nature — always keep the original and compress a copy for publishing.

Does it support PNG with transparency?

Transparency survives PNG output; converting to JPEG erases it since the format lacks alpha.
