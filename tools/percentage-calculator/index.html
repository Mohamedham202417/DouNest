Detailed explanation

Percentages are the official language of daily life: store discounts, taxes and fees, bank interest, sales growth, exam results — and calculation confusion repeats at checkout counters and in meetings.

The three calculation types cover every real question: «how much is 15% of 240?» (discount), «35 is what percent of 140?» (share), «from 80 to 95 is what increase?» (growth) — each type has a different equation and the tool picks it right.

Relative change is the most important in reports: from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase (not 20% as many think — the baseline is the starting point!) — this mix-up produces wrong corporate reports daily.

For smart shopping: two successive discounts of 20% then 10% are not 30% but 28% — the second applies to the discounted price. The tool helps verify such advertising promises instantly.

Live results while typing make the tool ideal for quick repeated calculations: price lists, offer comparisons, invoices — without opening a full calculator.

FAQ

How do I calculate percentage increase?

(new − old) ÷ old × 100 — old is always the denominator, not new.

Do successive discounts add up?

No — 20% then 10% = 28%, not 30%; the second applies to the post-discount price. Try it in the tool.

What if the result is negative?

In change calculations it means a decrease — mind the comparison direction when reading it.
