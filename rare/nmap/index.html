Nmap — The Famous Network Scanner

A curated rare tool — full explanation, getting-started steps and hashtags.

Ethical Hacking & Security Free & open source

⚠️ These tools are intended for ethical penetration testing on systems you have explicit permission to test. Using them on systems you are not authorized to access is a crime in most countries.

DouNest does not host or interactively run any tool here — these are information pages linking to the official projects only.

What is this tool?

Nmap is the most famous network scanner in history and the foundation of any serious penetration test: it discovers live hosts, open ports, running services and even the approximate operating system of each device. First released in 1997, it is still actively developed and ships hundreds of ready-made vulnerability-check scripts through its NSE engine.

Why we recommend it

Every ethical hacker's journey starts with Nmap; learning to read its output gives you a deep understanding of networks and how an attacker sees your infrastructure. Network admins also use it to find unauthorized devices and audit what is really exposed on their servers.

How to get started

  1. Download Nmap from the official site nmap.org and install it (Windows, Linux and macOS are supported).
  2. Build a personal lab: a virtual machine or your own router that you have explicit permission to test.
  3. Start simple: nmap -sV 192.168.1.1 to scan ports and services on your own host.
  4. Read the output: each line shows an open port, the service name and its probable version.
  5. Try advanced flags like nmap -A for a deep scan, and always document your findings in a written report.
