# مركز الحاسبات: direct competitor evidence memo

**Research checked:** 16 October 2026. Scope: online-calculator products Omni Calculator, Calculator.net, Desmos and Google Search calculator. Findings are based on the official product/help pages listed below. “Observed” distinguishes source evidence from recommendations. Device rendering, accessibility, and regional availability were not comprehensively tested.

## Competitor findings

### Omni Calculator
- **Coverage (observed):** Homepage taxonomy spans finance, conversion, everyday life, health, math and other categories. Reviewed representative tools include BMI, age in years/months/days and compound interest/percentage listings.
- **Depth (observed):** BMI page explains `BMI = weight / height²`, thresholds and limitations, cites sources, identifies creators/reviewers, and links related specialist calculators. Age tool takes date of birth and a configurable current date, supports past/future dates, and returns years/months/days. It notes the hand-calculation explanation is approximate while the tool result is exact.
- **Workflow and friction (observed):** BMI fields recalculate interactively and can be locked; the article explains the lock behavior. Rich editorial material, authorship and related links support trust and learning, but long text can add scanning burden for quick tasks.
- **Free/limits:** Reviewed calculators did not show a paywall or per-use limit; pages promoted a paid/custom-tool product. No universal pricing, ad-free, or geographic constraint was established.

### Calculator.net
- **Coverage (observed):** Homepage says it has around 200 calculators spanning finance, fitness, health, math and more. Explicitly says calculators are free and require no registration; also warns some are country-specific, citing US income tax.
- **Depth (observed):** BMI has US/metric/other-unit tabs, age and gender inputs, multiple outputs and formula/limitations. Percentage page separates common-phrase, percentage-difference and change tasks and explains formulas. Finance page has FV/PMT/IY/N/PV modes, settings, schedule and a time-value-of-money walkthrough.
- **Workflow/friction (observed):** Form-heavy pages often require a Calculate action. Percentage tasks ask for two values to solve a third; finance relies on abbreviations. Detailed explanations add value but can be dense. Broad categories and site search help navigation.
- **Free/limits:** Explicitly free/no registration per homepage; some tools have jurisdictional scope. No paid tier or usage limit was identified in reviewed pages.

### Desmos
- **Coverage (observed):** Official site offers scientific and graphing calculators, rather than a documented library of dedicated age/BMI/finance workflows. Scientific calculator includes percentages and mathematical expression entry.
- **Strength/accessibility (observed):** Help documents arithmetic, trig, roots, fractions, exponents, keyboard shortcuts, `ans`, complex mode, decimal-to-fraction conversion, Braille (UEB and Nemeth), and reverse contrast. Product presents the web scientific calculator as free.
- **Friction (observed):** Expression/keypad workspace expects users to formulate the math themselves; it does not guide domain-specific inputs or explain applied assumptions in the manner of a dedicated calculator article. Help is separate from the compact calculator surface.
- **Free/limits:** Reviewed web product/help describe it as free; no web calculator usage cap found. This does not establish terms for other Desmos services or partnerships.

### Google Search calculator
- **Coverage/workflow (observed):** Google Search Help says users can enter equations or search “Calculator,” and can type unit conversions or search “Unit converter.” It documents graphing equations and geometry calculator too. Query-first interaction is low-friction for simple one-off calculations. The same page documents color-code conversion.
- **Limits (observed):** The reviewed help page does not document dedicated BMI, age or finance workflows or explanatory formulas. This is a limit of documented positioning, not proof that no such query can ever return an answer. The page includes troubleshooting for calculator not appearing.
- **Free/geography:** No price is stated in reviewed help. Search answer module appearance can vary; availability was not tested across countries/devices, so avoid assuming uniform behavior.

## Practical comparison and DouNest opportunities

**Observed gap:** Omni and Calculator.net combine many purpose-built calculators with editorial explanation, but users must find the right dedicated page. Desmos is a powerful mathematical workspace and Google a quick query answer surface; their reviewed materials do not document guided common age/BMI/finance workflows.

**Recommendations (not observations):**
1. Create a searchable, task-language catalog for BMI, age, percentage, unit and finance, reachable from a simple home/category page.
2. Make inputs explicit and forgiving: visible units, localized examples, sensible defaults, clear result/reset/share controls and instant updates where appropriate. Explain finance terms (PV/FV/PMT) in ordinary language and surface timing/compounding assumptions.
3. Present a concise formula, worked substitution, assumptions and result interpretation beside the result; let users open deeper explanation without forcing a long article first. State health limitations and finance/jurisdiction scope.
4. Treat mobile and accessibility as product requirements: responsive touch targets, keyboard and screen-reader labels, contrast controls and uncluttered layouts. Desmos documented Braille/reverse contrast; use as a positive benchmark, not as evidence others lack these features.
5. Keep basic use account-free and disclose cost, ads, data handling and regional constraints plainly. Do not imply universal coverage where standards or jurisdictions differ.
