Detailed explanation

Unit conversion is a global daily operation: recipes in ounces and pounds while your scale reads grams, flights in miles while maps show kilometers, cloud storage in gigabytes while your report speaks megabytes — systemic gaps between standards never end.

Temperature is special and deserves attention: converting is not multiplying by a factor but shift-and-scale equations (F = C × 1.8 + 32) — naive generators get it wrong, the tool handles it correctly.

Data units carry their own trap: the difference between 1024 (binary — mebibytes) and 1000 (decimal — megabytes) creates a 10% gap on large drives that is constantly misunderstood — the tool clarifies which system is used.

Number precision is intentional: results display with sensible rounding instead of endless decimals, while full precision stays internal for chained calculations.

Factors follow official international standards — for legal or critical engineering conversions always verify against your country's official reference.

FAQ

Are conversions 100% accurate?

Factors are official standards; display is sensibly rounded with full precision internally — plenty for any practical use.

Why do data units differ?

Two systems: binary (1024) technical, decimal (1000) marketing — the gap explains the mystery of a «1TB drive showing 931GB».

Are old imperial units supported?

The common ones are supported (mile, yard, pound, ounce) — suggest rare units for addition.
