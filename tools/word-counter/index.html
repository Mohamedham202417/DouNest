How to use

  1. Paste your text or article into the large input box.
  2. Read the live stats: words, characters, sentences and paragraphs.
  3. Follow the estimated reading time (in minutes) based on 200 words per minute.
  4. Use the density info to see your most repeated words.
  5. Edit the text directly if you like — stats update with every keystroke.

Detailed explanation

A word counter is not a luxury; word limits are hard rules in university submissions, SEO briefs, executive summaries and job application letters — and manually tracking them on long text is practically impossible.

The estimated reading timer is a powerful editorial tool: a good web article sits between 900 and 1300 words (5-7 minutes), while a press release should read in one minute — the numbers steer you immediately.

Character counts in all three forms (with and without spaces) matter on strict platforms like X/Twitter and abstract fields in submission forms.

Sentence and paragraph counts expose structural problems before readers see them: one paragraph the length of the whole piece, or 40-word average sentences telling you the text needs breathing room.

Everything updates as you type with no refresh button — an experience enabling real editing rather than repeated paste-check cycles.

FAQ

How does it count Arabic words?

It splits on spaces with full Arabic morphology support, matching word-processor counts.

Is the reading estimate accurate for technical text?

Dense text reads slower (~150 wpm) — treat the figure as a floor and add 20% for technical content.

Is my text saved anywhere?

No — analysis stays in local memory only; refreshing the page clears the text for good.
