How to use

  4. Review the conversion in both your local timezone and UTC.
  5. Copy the result in the format you need: seconds or milliseconds.

Detailed explanation

A Unix timestamp is the count of seconds since January 1, 1970 UTC — the shared time language of all systems: databases, APIs, logs, filesystems — all store time as this timezone-neutral number.

The most famous practical trap is seconds versus milliseconds: a 10-digit timestamp is seconds, 13 digits is milliseconds — pasting the wrong type gives you 1970 or the year 55000! The tool auto-detects the type and handles both.

Reverse conversion matters for building and testing: generate a timestamp for a demo expiry date, or one unified moment across timezones for a global team — «the meeting at timestamp 1730000000» is unambiguous.

Timezones are the second headache: the same timestamp shows noon in Cairo and morning in New York — the dual local/UTC display prevents confusion in both cases.

Daily uses: diagnosing system logs, checking JWT validity (the exp field is a timestamp!), synchronizing dates across systems — a small tool earning a permanent tab.

FAQ

How do I tell seconds from milliseconds?

Count the digits: 10 = seconds, 13 = milliseconds; the tool auto-detects and converts correctly.

Why 1970 specifically?

A historical Unix decision — the «Epoch» became a world standard inherited by all modern systems.

Does it support dates before 1970?

Yes, via negative timestamps — useful for historical data in legacy systems.
