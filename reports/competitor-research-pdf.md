# DouNest competitor memo: online PDF tool centers

**Scope:** PDF24 Tools, iLovePDF, Smallpdf, and Adobe Acrobat online; publicly accessible product, pricing, and help pages checked 16 October 2026. This memo assesses the central tool-discovery experience and common tasks, not the vendors’ desktop suites or enterprise products. Tool pages expose a credible intended workflow; an exhaustive live upload-to-download usability test was not performed.

## Evidence-based comparison

### PDF24 Tools
The center is a dense, broad catalog: the landing page links directly to merge, split, compress, edit, sign, conversion, rotate/remove/extract/rearrange pages, OCR, redaction, and utilities. Merge offers thumbnail/page selection, drag-and-drop file ordering, page-mode options, bookmarks, and blank-page insertion. OCR exposes language choice, PDF/PDF-A output, deskew/cleanup/background removal, and combine-files controls. This breadth and hands-on control is a strength for direct utility tasks. [1][2][3]

PDF24 says all tools are free, without artificial limits or registration, financed by web advertising; the center and OCR page visibly include repeated “Advertisement” placements. It says files are encrypted in transit and deleted after a short time, but the pages do not specify a precise retention interval. It supports smartphones and major browsers and offers an offline Windows Creator alternative. The tradeoff is a busy, ad-heavy catalog and somewhat utilitarian visual hierarchy; users must pick among many discrete tools rather than being guided by intent. [1][2]

### iLovePDF
The tool center is task-led, and merge’s focused page explicitly supports drag-and-drop ordering and file selection; Drive/Dropbox import is advertised in help. The pricing comparison documents merge, split, compress, Office conversions, OCR, reorder/organize, sign, and many other tasks. Free Basic does not mean unlimited: published per-task batch counts differ by operation (for example, merge 25 files, compress 2, OCR 1), and size caps vary (e.g. merge/split 100 MB, OCR 15 MB). Premium lists up to 4 GB for many tasks and 10-file task batches for common workflows; some tasks still have lower ceilings. [4][5][6]

The official pages describe encryption and automatic deletion within two hours (and manual delete option); the security page says signed documents may be retained up to five years. Basic has limited tools/processing, ads, and web-only access in the comparison; Premium is ad-free and adds web/mobile/desktop, unlimited processing, and regional processing. Price is shown as US$5/month billed annually or US$9 monthly on the retrieved page, but displayed price/tax can vary by region and billing. Strong pricing transparency is a plus, although detailed caps and many feature rows make comparison dense. [4][5][6]

### Smallpdf
Smallpdf’s landing and merge pages emphasize a curated set of popular tools, then expose a broad categorized tool directory (compress, convert/OCR, organize, edit, sign, protect, AI, scan). Merge documents preview, reorder, rotate, delete pages, and accept PDFs, images, Word and Office files; the page claims automatic server deletion after one hour. Mobile is positioned as an app for scanning, organizing and sharing. [7][8]

Its pricing page says Free includes 30+ tools but limited downloads, limited app access, and daily limits on many task types; Pro adds unlimited tool/download access, OCR/edit text, strong compression and unlimited mobile access. A seven-day free trial is promoted. The price was ellipsized in the retrieved public page, so no numeric rate is asserted. Free task ceilings are described as “daily download limit” rather than a single universal number in the plan comparison. This makes broad access easy to sample but recurring use or advanced OCR/strong compression an upgrade trigger. Main page and merge page make privacy/security claims and list ISO/IEC 27001 and GDPR/CCPA/nFADP compliance; claims are vendor-stated. [7][8]

### Adobe Acrobat online
Adobe organizes a large suite by job family (convert, reduce file size, edit, sign/protect) and includes prominent AI tools alongside classic operations. The center lists merge, split, reorder, compression, conversion, OCR, and fill/sign. Merge allows up to 100 files and 1,500 pages total (500 pages per input), reorders files before combining, and says no watermark/subscription; Adobe distinguishes file-level ordering before merge from individual page reorganization, which requires sign-in. Split is a clear counterexample to “free without a gate”: it requires sign-in after upload, supports up to 20 output files/19 dividers, and documents a 1 GB/1,500-page input ceiling. [9][10][11]

Adobe says uploaded files are deleted unless users sign in and save them. Its content is excellent at explaining steps and concrete use cases; long SEO pages create more reading before the action, while account/cloud-save prompts are part of the workflow. The main center calls the tools free, but task-specific gates and limits matter more than that blanket label. Adobe pages say cross-device/browser availability, including phones; actual mobile UI ergonomics were not independently tested. Pricing and access can vary by locale/product offer. [9][10][11]

## Cross-competitor findings

- **Capability coverage:** All four publicly list merge, split, compression, conversion, signing, and page organization. OCR is present at all four, but capability and free access differ: PDF24 has extensive OCR settings; iLovePDF lists OCR as available on Basic but imposes a one-file/15 MB free task cap; Smallpdf lists OCR among tools but marks OCR/advanced features as limited or Pro; Adobe offers free searchable-PDF OCR but requires sign-in to download according to its OCR FAQ. Do not treat “OCR” as equivalent to editable OCR-to-Office across vendors. [2][4][7][9][12]
- **Center experience:** PDF24 wins on visible breadth and openly stated no-registration/no-limit access, with ads as the explicit funding mechanism. iLovePDF exposes unusually granular limits/pricing. Smallpdf presents a more curated home and practical previews/mobile positioning but funnels repeat/advanced use toward Pro. Adobe’s grouping and task pages are polished and content-rich, with account save/download gates on some tasks. These are observed design/claim differences, not a universal ranking. [1][4][7][9]
- **Privacy:** All cloud tools require upload for server processing. Retention claims are not directly comparable: PDF24 says “short time” without a precise interval on these pages; iLovePDF specifies two hours (with a distinct five-year signed-document exception); Smallpdf merge states one hour; Adobe says deletion unless signed in and saved. These are vendor statements, not independent security audits. Offer clear just-in-time retention disclosure and a local/offline option rather than implying cloud tools are risk-free. [1][2][5][8][9]
- **Monetization friction:** PDF24 places ads in the task center; iLovePDF and Smallpdf monetize caps/paid tiers (and explicitly offer ad-free paid use at iLovePDF); Adobe uses identity/account and cloud-storage progression in certain tasks. All create different forms of friction; avoid promising unrestricted free use without a sustainable policy. [1][4][7][10][11]
- **Mobile/discovery/content:** All claim browser/mobile compatibility or provide mobile apps; Smallpdf and iLovePDF explicitly merchandise mobile apps, while PDF24 lists phone support and Adobe claims any device. This is marketing/documentation evidence, not hands-on mobile testing. Each invests in indexable, operation-specific instructional pages and FAQs. PDF24 has especially broad tool-link cross-promotion; Adobe has high explanatory depth; iLovePDF publishes useful cap tables; Smallpdf’s helpful merge guide explains supported mixed formats. [1][4][7][8][9]

## Prioritized, credible opportunities for DouNest (recommendations)

1. **Fast “task center” with intent-first grouping and search.** Keep the core actions (merge, split, reorder, compress, convert, sign, OCR) visible immediately, then group secondary utilities. Show a task-specific one-sentence outcome and step preview. This pairs PDF24’s discoverability breadth with Smallpdf’s curated entry points without a crowded directory.
2. **Make page control the differentiator.** Provide a coherent shared page workspace: thumbnail preview, drag reorder, select/remove/extract, rotate, and merge mixed files, then continue to compress/convert/sign without restarting. PDF24 and Smallpdf demonstrate preview/reorder utility; Adobe documents that file ordering and page reorganization may be separate or gated.
3. **Publish exact free limits beside the action.** State per-task file count, size, page ceiling, daily quota, sign-in requirement, and any OCR/strong-compression restriction before upload. Keep a live, concise comparison page. Competitor limits are task-specific and can be obscured by “free” headline claims; iLovePDF’s detailed table is a useful transparency benchmark.
4. **A trustworthy privacy choice at upload.** Show where processing occurs, precise deletion period, whether an account is needed to download/save, and a delete-now action. Where feasible, provide local processing for privacy-sensitive operations or make the offline option discoverable. Never claim zero-risk or imply a third-party assurance that has not been obtained.
5. **Mobile-first task flows and useful SEO help.** Design touch-sized upload/reorder/download interactions, not only responsive marketing pages. Pair every tool with concise instructions, supported input/output formats, examples, troubleshooting, and accessible FAQs; link adjacent next actions. Validate on real mobile browsers before claiming parity.
6. **Offer a clear free-use model.** Choose and explain the tradeoff—ads, daily caps, or a small paid plan—rather than interrupting mid-task. If a gate is unavoidable, disclose it before upload and preserve the user’s work when prompting.
