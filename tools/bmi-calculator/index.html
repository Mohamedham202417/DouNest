Detailed explanation

Body Mass Index is the world's most widely used health triage tool: your weight in kilograms divided by your height in meters squared, classifying you into ranges: underweight, normal, overweight, obese — simple numbers with broad health meaning.

The globally adopted standard ranges: below 18.5 underweight, 18.5-24.9 normal, 25-29.9 overweight, 30+ obese — the gaps between them are small but their population-level health significance is huge.

Honest limits: it is statistical sorting for populations, not individual diagnosis; a dense-muscle athlete may show «obese» despite peak fitness, and seniors may show «normal» despite muscle loss — use it as a compass, not a verdict.

The real value is the trend, not the number: tracking BMI monthly reveals your weight's direction before the scale alone shows it — a 2-point rise over a year is a stronger signal than a static figure.

Pair it with other indicators for the full picture: waist circumference, body fat percentage, activity level — health is multiple numbers, not one; BMI starts the conversation with your doctor, it does not end it.

FAQ

Is BMI suitable for children?

Not with adult ranges — children need age and sex specific percentiles evaluated with medical growth charts.

Why do I show obese as an athlete?

Muscle is denser than fat; the index cannot tell them apart — body composition measurements suit athletic cases better.

What is the healthy weight for my height?

Try hypothetical weights until the index lands in 18.5-24.9 — the tool shows it instantly.
